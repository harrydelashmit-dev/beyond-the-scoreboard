# Beyond the Scoreboard: Does University Investment in Athletics Pay Off?
AC 547 — Track A: Analyze

**What is the question/problem?** When does athletics revenue growth keep up with spending, and who carries the remaining financial burden? We examine dollar cost recovery, differences by school type and prior support dependence, whether success reduces actual support, and whether statistical football outperformance benefits smaller-budget programs more. We connect this CFO question to the original spending → competitive success → revenue/student demand pathway, comparing Directors' Cup performance with football and nationally visible men's basketball success. Every link is a hypothesis rather than an assumed payoff.

**Why is it important?** Athletics competes with academic programs and other university priorities for limited resources. An athletic department's reported revenue can include university support and student fees, so an apparently balanced budget does not necessarily indicate financial self-sufficiency. Athletic visibility might also create institutional benefits, but more applicants do not automatically produce additional students or tuition contribution. Administrators need evidence about these distinctions before using athletics as an investment or recruitment strategy.

**How will we address it?** Python combines registered Knight-Newhouse finances, EADA sport spending, NACDA standings, ESPN historical ratings/records/polls, NCES IPEDS institutional outcomes and BLS CPI-U for 2010–2019. Audited school-year joins support constant-dollar changes, school/conference-year models, direct group interactions, school-clustered confidence intervals and multiple-testing adjustments. The completed historical pass finds a consistent football–ticket association and incomplete primary dollar cost recovery, especially among historically support-dependent programs. Student-demand and smaller-program breakthrough advantages remain uncertain. A matched-endpoint accounting comparison distinguishes support dollars from support percentages. All planned results and contrary sensitivity findings are retained. Hypothetical break-even/NPV scenarios separate applicants, intake and enrolled stock; neither the correlations nor the accounting proxies identify causal cash ROI.
