The central financial result is +6.41% next-year all-sports ticket receipts per 10 FPI points (95% interval +3.99% to +8.89%; 817 school-years, 109 schools), an observational association passing the financial-family adjustment. Do not equate it with a return on extra spending. The original 52-school 3.2% claim is not numerically replicated. Student-demand results are mixed; the basketball annual-change signal is exploratory, and the favorable school-trend result is sample-sensitive. A smaller p-value for football does not establish a larger causal/per-dollar return than another sport.

The new core result is approximately $0.64 revenue growth per spending dollar (95% CI $0.50–$0.79; 2,002 school-years, 227 schools), and $0.68 for three-year cumulative budget flows. The primary null is dollar-for-dollar coverage, not zero correlation. High-baseline-support programs have a lower recovery slope ($0.30 versus $0.70), with a direct adjusted group difference. Smaller budget and Power Five contrasts do not clear the chosen primary adjustment. None of the six primary support-response tests passes adjustment. The hypothesized smaller-program outperformance advantage is not established. Negative exploratory AP interactions must be disclosed and kept in context rather than silently dropped.

A concrete accounting example: 222 matched programs' real support dollars rose 31.7% over 2010–2019 even though support/expenses fell from 31.2% to 28.0%. A lower percentage can conceal a larger dollar obligation. Educational, participation and other mission benefits are outside this financial measurement; incomplete commercial recovery is not proof those benefits have no value.

Main figures: `feasibility_pooled_within`, `feasibility_financial_returns`, `returns_cost_recovery`, `returns_school_types`, `returns_support_dollars_share`. Keep `returns_self_support`, `returns_breakthrough`, student-demand and scenario charts for discussion/backup if time is tight. Explain the raw-versus-adjusted p-value distinction once, then focus on effect sizes and confidence intervals. Show the contrary school/year level sensitivity whose interval includes dollar-for-dollar recovery; the change-model result is not universal across every specification.

Use generated charts at full size. Explain that a confidence interval crossing zero means the sample does not clearly distinguish positive and negative associations under the model. Do not describe insignificance as proof of no effect. Present exact sample sizes and avoid using all-DI counts for a smaller regression sample.
