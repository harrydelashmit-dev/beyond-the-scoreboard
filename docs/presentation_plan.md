Use the expanded feasibility analysis as the evidence source. Suggested slides: title with five names; CFO question and proposed pathway; source/timing audit; pooled versus within-school correlations; primary spending-success estimates; football versus other success measures and next-year receipts; primary applications/enrollment intervals; exploratory Sweet16 growth result with same-case sensitivity; qualified accounting measure; break-even/cohort/capacity scenarios; limitations and decision implications; references. Put the complete regression grid and source details in backup slides.

The central financial result is +6.41% next-year all-sports ticket receipts per 10 FPI points (95% interval +3.99% to +8.89%; 817 school-years, 109 schools), an observational association passing the financial-family adjustment. Do not equate it with a return on extra spending. The original 52-school 3.2% claim is not numerically replicated. Student-demand results are mixed; the basketball annual-change signal is exploratory, and the favorable school-trend result is sample-sensitive. A smaller p-value for football does not establish a larger causal/per-dollar return than another sport.

Main figures: `feasibility_pooled_within`, `feasibility_financial_returns`, `feasibility_student_demand`, and `feasibility_cfo_break_even`. Keep the two robustness figures for discussion/backup if time is tight. Explain the raw-versus-adjusted p-value distinction once, then focus on effect sizes and confidence intervals.

Use generated charts at full size. Explain that a confidence interval crossing zero means the sample does not clearly distinguish positive and negative associations under the model. Do not describe insignificance as proof of no effect. Present exact sample sizes and avoid using all-DI counts for a smaller regression sample.

Before final slides: have someone outside the group interpret the charts, record confusion, fix it, rehearse to 18–22 minutes, consult the instructor, and determine the assigned December 7 or December 9 slot. Commit the final slides in this repository. Instructor sharing and group evaluations remain human actions.
